Privacy policy

Last updated: 26 October 2026

This policy covers My eBird Map and eBird Trip Planner, independent birding tools operated by Raphaël Nussbaumer. For privacy questions or requests, email rafnuss@gmail.com.

My eBird Map processes your export in your browser, with optional backups in your own Google Drive. eBird Trip Planner also works with browser data; its optional cloud features store trips in the app’s Google Firebase project and let you share them with others.

1. Information you choose to use

Imported files and trip content may contain observer names or identifiers, observation dates, precise locations, checklist identifiers, species records, comments, personal bird lists, travel dates, routes and notes. The information processed depends on the files you import and the features you use. You do not need to supply information unrelated to those features. Please avoid importing or sharing personal information about other people without permission or another lawful basis.

2. My eBird Map

Your imported CSV or ZIP is read and processed in your browser. The source file, processed observations and import metadata are cached in IndexedDB, and display preferences are stored locally. Importing a new dataset replaces the previous local snapshot. The app does not upload your observation export to an app-operated database.

When you choose Google Drive backup or restore, Google asks you to authorise drive.appdata. This permission lets the app manage its own hidden app-data folder in your Google account; it does not let the app browse your ordinary Drive files. The app uploads the full source export, compressed where appropriate, with metadata including the filename, import time, backup time and format. A backup includes the full export regardless of map or species filters.

Backups are transferred directly between your browser and Google Drive. They belong to your Google account, use your Google storage allowance and are hidden from the normal Drive file list. Each save creates a separate backup, older backups are retained, and restore uses the latest completed backup. There is no automatic background synchronisation. Google access tokens are kept in browser memory rather than saved by the app for later sessions.

3. eBird Trip Planner

Local planning and optional Google sign-in

Trip data, imported datasets, lists and planning preferences are stored in your browser for local use. If you choose Google sign-in, Firebase Authentication processes your Google account identifier and basic profile information, such as your email address, name and profile image when supplied by Google. Your account identity and email are used to associate cloud trips with you, manage access and identify trips shared with you. The browser remembers your Firebase sign-in until you sign out or clear its stored session. Firebase account records remain until deleted; signing out does not delete your account.

Cloud trips

Cloud-enabled trips store imported source data, personal lists, itinerary content and associated metadata in the app-operated Firebase project using Cloud Storage and Firestore. Signed-in trip creation enables cloud saving by default, and subsequent itinerary changes can synchronise automatically, including when you reconnect after working offline. Cloud metadata includes owner identifiers and email, sharing permissions, revisions and activity timestamps.

Trip Planner cloud data is stored in the app’s Firebase project, rather than in your personal Google Drive. Trip files are stored in a Cloud Storage bucket in South Carolina, United States (us-east1), and Firestore trip metadata is stored in the United States multi-region (nam5). Authorised administrators can access cloud data for support, security and service operation. Cloud access is governed by authentication and access rules; cloud trips are not end-to-end encrypted.

Sharing and additional features

If you enable public viewing, anyone with the viewing link can access the shared trip without signing in. Treat the trip’s source data, lists, notes and itinerary as potentially disclosed to recipients. Invited editors sign in with Google and can modify shared trips; their email addresses are stored to manage access. Turning off sharing prevents future authorised access through the app, but cannot recall copies already downloaded or exported.

If you request more cloud capacity, the app stores your account identifier, email address, requested capacity, explanation, request status and update time for administrator review. If you load an eBird trip report, the requested report identifier and, where needed, species identifiers are sent to the trip-report service at tripreport.raphaelnussbaumer.com to retrieve the report data.

4. Maps, hosting and analytics

Both apps use Mapbox for maps. Mapbox receives connection information such as your IP address and requests for map resources. In Trip Planner, place searches and route calculations also send the search text or relevant route coordinates to Mapbox. If you enable the browser’s location feature, your location is used to position the map. Displaying observation markers does not itself require uploading the full observation dataset to Mapbox.

The sites are hosted on GitHub Pages. GitHub and other providers serving requested resources may process technical information, including IP addresses, request URLs, browser information and access times, for delivery, security and service operation. Clicking an external eBird or other link sends you to that provider, whose privacy practices then apply.

My eBird Map does not currently include Google Analytics. Trip Planner currently loads a separate Google Analytics website tag, although its Firebase project has no linked Analytics integration. It uses Google Analytics to measure site use. Analytics may process page URLs, referrers, browser and device information, approximate location and cookie or similar identifiers. Its current implementation loads the analytics script when the page opens; there is no in-app analytics consent control. Browser privacy tools may limit this processing.

Provider information: GitHub privacy statement, Mapbox privacy policy, Google privacy policy and Firebase privacy information.

The Firebase project also enables Google’s optional use of Firebase Service Data to analyse use of and improve non-Firebase Google services. Firebase Service Data concerns Google’s provision and administration of Firebase and excludes the customer data stored in trips. See the Firebase privacy information above for Google’s description of this processing.

5. Purposes and use of Google data

Information is used to display observations, build and restore trips, provide optional account and cloud features, carry out sharing choices, review capacity requests, answer support requests and maintain service security. Trip Planner analytics is used to understand site use. Personal observations and trip content are not sold, used for advertising, or used to train general-purpose AI models.

The apps’ use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including its Limited Use requirements. Google-derived user data is used for the app features described here. It is shared only as needed to provide those features at your direction, with service providers supporting them, for security, or where required by law.

6. Retention and deletion

  • Browser data: remains until replaced, deleted through available app controls, cleared in browser settings or removed by the browser. Clearing site data for this origin may affect both apps. The operator cannot remotely erase data stored only on your device.
  • My eBird Map backups: remain in your Google Drive until deleted. In Drive, open Settings → Manage apps → My eBird Map → Options → Delete hidden app data. You can revoke the app’s access in your Google account’s third-party connections settings. Revoking access does not itself delete existing backups.
  • Trip Planner cloud trips: remain while you use cloud storage, unless deleted by you or removed as part of service administration. Trips inactive for more than six months may be removed after administrator review; this is not a guarantee of either six months’ retention or automatic deletion at six months. Keep exported backups.
  • Cloud deletion: use Cloud actions → Delete cloud trip to remove a cloud trip and stop its sharing. This keeps the browser copy. Deleted Storage objects can remain recoverable under the bucket’s current seven-day soft-delete policy. The bucket has no automatic lifecycle deletion rules, and Firestore has no time-to-live expiry policies. Temporary uploads, recovery copies, account records and capacity requests may require separate cleanup; contact the operator for a complete deletion request.
  • Other records: support correspondence and capacity requests are kept while needed to resolve the request and administer the service. Technical logs and analytics records follow the relevant provider and project retention settings. Contact the operator for information about the settings applying to your request.

Signing out, clearing browser data or revoking Google sign-in does not automatically delete Trip Planner cloud data. Contact the operator to request deletion of your Firebase account and remaining app-managed personal information.

7. Your choices and rights

You can use local features without Google sign-in. Optional Drive access, cloud storage and sharing require the information needed to provide those features. You can stop using those features, revoke Google access, change sharing permissions and delete copies as described above.

Where applicable data-protection law applies, providing the features you request is based on performing the service agreement; security, abuse prevention and support rely on the legitimate interest in running a reliable service. Processing subject to a consent requirement must rely on valid consent; agreeing to these terms or using an optional feature is not blanket consent to unrelated tracking.

Depending on applicable law, you may request access, correction, deletion, restriction or portability of your personal information, object to processing based on legitimate interests, and withdraw consent where processing relies on it. Email rafnuss@gmail.com; identity verification may be needed to protect your information. You can complain to your local data-protection authority, including the CNIL where relevant. The apps do not make automated decisions producing legal or similarly significant effects about you.

8. International processing and changes

Google, Firebase, Mapbox and GitHub operate internationally, so information may be processed outside your country, including in the United States. Applicable transfer arrangements and safeguards depend on the provider and service agreement. Contact the operator for details of arrangements applying to app-managed cloud data.

This policy will be updated when features or data practices change. The date above identifies the latest revision. Material changes affecting how Google user data is used will be disclosed before introducing the new use, and additional consent will be requested where required.
